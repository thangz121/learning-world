# SayBananas — Technical Evidence

## Template-matching architecture (E2, Syracuse handout)
- Before gameplay: users record a **"correct" template and an "incorrect" template** for each
  word.
- During play: child's recording compared to templates → whichever it is more similar to
  determines feedback ("Good job!" / "Not quite").
- Findings: algorithm agreed with expert clinicians more than parent feedback; **fewer than half
  of recordings analyzable due to audio quality issues**.

## Effectiveness study (E2, Int J Speech-Language Pathology / CSU)
- 45 rural AU children (4;4–10;5), SSD, 4 weeks, 10–15 target words, motor learning principles.
- Usage/performance automatically monitored; median 44.7 trials/session (~45% of 100 target).
- Gains on treated words (median +15.66%); sessions correlated with improvement; no reliable
  change on parent-rated intelligibility.
- Children rated app "happy/good/fun".

## Game design principles (E2, UNH thesis)
- Stars mandatory to collect → speech exercises happen during gameplay (not after).
- Feedback options for caregiver: correct / move on / try again; **max one retry per word**
  (avoid practicing errors).
- KR (knowledge of results) / KP (knowledge of performance) framing; parent training day.

## Relevance to LWE
- Template matching is a low-resource alternative to phoneme-level scoring (personalised per
  child, no big model) — and its ~50% audio-quality attrition is direct external evidence for
  our assessability problem.
